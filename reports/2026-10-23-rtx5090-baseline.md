# Baseline: Qwen3.5-4B FP8 on vLLM 0.32.0, one RTX 5090, in-process

The engine alone (`bench/inproc/run.py`): pre-tokenized random prompts of 1000 tokens, all
submitted at once, `ignore_eos`; vLLM defaults otherwise (async scheduling, FULL_AND_PIECEWISE
CUDA graphs, chunked prefill at 8192 tokens, 256 sequences), 0.55 of the card. Checkpoint
`RedHatAI/Qwen3.5-4B-FP8-dynamic`. Captured with Nsight Systems over the measured run
(`bench/profile/inproc_nsys.sh`), summarised over the steady window (the kernel span without its
first and last 10%) by `bench/profile/nsys_summary.py`.

## Reading it

- **The checkpoint is a third BF16.** `RedHatAI/Qwen3.5-4B-FP8-dynamic` ignores every
  `linear_attn.*` projection of the 24 GDN layers (in_proj_qkv, in_proj_z, in_proj_a, in_proj_b,
  out_proj, ~1.0 B parameters), the vision tower and `lm_head` (tied, 248320 x 2560). Those run
  as BF16 GEMMs on cuBLAS's sm80 kernels: 25-29% of all GPU time. From the token rates and the
  parameter counts, the FP8 GEMMs run near 300 TFLOP/s while they run and the BF16 ones near
  175; the BF16 slice is large because of its precision, and the first lever on it is FP8
  (half the time and the weight traffic), then the kernel (a Triton / Gluon GEMM for the
  decode-sized shapes, where cuBLAS's sm80 tiles are the weakest).
- **GDN decode is state-bound.** The recurrent state is float32 (`mamba_ssm_dtype`), 32 value heads
  x 128 x 128 per layer per sequence; the decode kernel reads and writes all of it every step,
  which is why it grows to 19.7% with long replies.
- **The GPU still waits 13-18% of the time** in gaps over 100 us: every step syncs on the host
  (`cudaEventSynchronize`, 1278 steps), and mixed prefill/decode steps run piecewise, launching
  ~460 kernels per step eagerly (12.7 s of launch calls in the 72.6 s window, short shape).

## Levers, in the order the numbers rank them

1. Mixed steps into full CUDA graphs (GDN with device-side metadata): the launch gaps.
2. GDN projections and `lm_head` in FP8 (precision checked against the BF16 reference's error
   profile), then GEMM kernels for decode shapes.
3. The GDN decode kernel: state traffic (layout, bf16 state as a checked option).
4. Attention: FlashAttention 2 split-KV at head dim 256 on sm120.
